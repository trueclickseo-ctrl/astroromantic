Understanding Tithi & Panjika in the Bengali Calendar

Unlike standard civil calendars, the Indian Bengali Panjika is deeply astronomical. Religious festivals such as Durga Puja, Kali Puja, and Kojagari Lakshmi Puja depend on exact Tithi (lunar day) transitions rather than fixed clock hours.

🟣 Ekadashi & FastingObserved on the 11th lunar day of Shukla and Krishna Pakshas.
🌕 Purnima & AmavasyaFull moon (Kojagari, Dol) and new moon (Mahalaya, Kali Puja) timings.
☀️ Sankranti TransitsMarks the end of each Bengali month as the Sun enters a new zodiac sign.

The 12 Bengali Months (১২ বাংলা মাস)

Month 1

Boishakh (বৈশাখ)

Summer (Grisma)
Gregorian: Mid-April – Mid-May

First month of Bangabda. Poila Boishakh, Haal Khata (new ledger), and Kalbaishakhi summer storms.

Month 2

Joishtho (জ্যৈষ্ঠ)

Summer (Grisma)
Gregorian: Mid-May – Mid-June

Peak summer. Season of juicy mangoes, lychees, Jamai Sasthi celebrations.

Month 3

Asharh (আষাঢ়)

Monsoon (Barsha)
Gregorian: Mid-June – Mid-July

Rains arrive. Inspiration for Rabindranath Tagore poems, Rath Yatra festival.

Month 4

Shrabon (শ্রাবণ)

Monsoon (Barsha)
Gregorian: Mid-July – Mid-August

Heavy monsoon. Sacred Shrabon Mondays for Lord Shiva, Manasa Puja, Jhulan Yatra.

Month 5

Bhadro (ভাদ্র)

Early Autumn (Sarat)
Gregorian: Mid-August – Mid-September

Kashphool begins blooming. Janmashtami, Tal Navami, Bhaluka Puja.

Month 6

Ashwin (আশ্বিন)

Autumn (Sarat)
Gregorian: Mid-September – Mid-October

Grandest month in Bengal. Mahalaya, Sharadiya Durga Puja, Kojagari Lakshmi Puja.

Month 7

Kartik (কার্তিক)

Late Autumn (Hemanta)
Gregorian: Mid-October – Mid-November

Kali Puja, Diwali lights, Jagaddhatri Puja, Kartik Puja, Bhaiphota (Bhai Dooj).

Month 8

Agrahayan (অগ্রহায়ণ)

Late Autumn (Hemanta)
Gregorian: Mid-November – Mid-December

Nabanna (new harvest festival), winter sweet date-palm jaggery (Nolen Gur).

Month 9

Poush (পৌষ)

Winter (Sit)
Gregorian: Mid-December – Mid-January

Poush Parbon, Pithe Puli making, Gangasagar Mela on Makar Sankranti.

Month 10

Magh (মাঘ)

Winter (Sit)
Gregorian: Mid-January – Mid-February

Saraswati Puja (Basant Panchami), yellow attire, spring warmth returning.

Month 11

Falgun (ফাল্গুন)

Spring (Basanta)
Gregorian: Mid-February – Mid-March

Dol Purnima (Holi), colorful Abir play, spring breeze.

Month 12

Chaitra (চৈত্র)

Spring (Basanta)
Gregorian: Mid-March – Mid-April

Chaitra Sale shopping, Charak Puja, Neel Shasthi, year-end Gajan rituals.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the Bengali calendar 2026–2027?▼

The Bengali calendar 2026–2027 is the traditional lunisolar regional calendar used across West Bengal, Tripura, and Indian Bengali communities. It covers Bangabda years 1433 and 1434, displaying Bengali months (Boishakh to Chaitra), Tithi, Ekadashi, and major Pujas.

What Bengali year (Bangabda) corresponds to 2026?▼

The year 2026 spans Bangabda 1433 (up to mid-April 2026) and Bangabda 1434 (beginning on Poila Boishakh / April 15, 2027).

When is Poila Boishakh in 2026?▼

Poila Boishakh (Bengali New Year 1433) in the Indian Bengali Panjika calendar falls on April 15, 2026.

What are the 12 Bengali months?▼

The 12 Bengali months are Boishakh (বৈশাখ), Joishtho (জ্যৈষ্ঠ), Asharh (আষাঢ়), Shrabon (শ্রাবণ), Bhadro (ভাদ), Ashwin (আশ্বিন), Kartik (কার্তিক), Agrahayan (অগ্রহায়ণ), Poush (পৌষ), Magh (মাঘ), Falgun (ফাল্গুন), and Chaitra (চৈত্র).

What is Bengali Panjika?▼

Bengali Panjika (পঞ্জিকা) is the traditional Hindu astronomical almanac that calculates auspicious timings, Tithi, Nakshatra, Yoga, Karana, Ekadashi dates, and ceremonial Puja schedules.

Why can Bengali festival dates differ by location?▼

Bengali festival dates depend on local solar sunrise/sunset and Tithi transition timings. Additionally, different Panjika traditions (such as Vishuddha Siddhanta vs Surya Siddhanta) may calculate exact Tithi boundaries with slight variations.

Is the Bengali calendar the same as the Bangladesh Bangla calendar?▼

No. The Indian Bengali calendar follows the traditional astronomical solar Panjika system where month lengths vary year to year. Bangladesh uses a revised civil calendar where month lengths and Pohela Boishakh (fixed on April 14) follow fixed civil rules.

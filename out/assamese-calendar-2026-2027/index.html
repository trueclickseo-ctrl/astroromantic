The Three Bihus of Assam (২০২৬–২০২৭)

Bihu is the soul of Assamese culture, marking three distinct stages of the agricultural cycle.

Spring • Rongali Bihu

Bohag Bihu (বহাগ বিহু)

Apr 14 – 16, 2026 | Apr 14 – 16, 2027

Marks the Assamese New Year (Bohag 1) and arrival of spring. Begins with Goru Bihu (cattle worship) followed by Manuh Bihu, Husori dance, and presenting woven Gamosas.

Autumn • Kongali Bihu

Kati Bihu (কাতি বিহু)

October 18, 2026 | October 18, 2027

Observed in Kati month when granaries are empty and paddy is growing. Families light earthen lamps (Saki) at the foot of Tulsi plants and in paddy fields for crop protection.

Winter • Bhogali Bihu

Magh Bihu (মাঘ বিহু)

Jan 14 – 15, 2026 | Jan 14 – 15, 2027

Post-harvest feast festival. Starts with Uruka community feasts inside Bhelaghar huts, followed by lighting tall Meji bonfires, pitha eating, and traditional games.

The 12 Assamese Months (অসমীয়া ১২ মাহ)

The Assamese calendar divides the year into 12 solar months based on solar transits (Sankranti).

Month 1

Bohag (বহাগ)

Spring (Basanta)
Gregorian: Mid-April – Mid-May

First month of Bhaskarabda. Celebrates Bohag Bihu (Rongali Bihu), welcoming spring, new agricultural cycle, and joy.

Month 2

Jeth (জেঠ)

Summer (Grisma)
Gregorian: Mid-May – Mid-June

Warm summer month. Associated with ripening summer fruits, Sabitri Brata, and pre-monsoon showers.

Month 3

Ahar (আহাৰ)

Monsoon (Barsa)
Gregorian: Mid-June – Mid-July

Arrival of monsoon rains. Paddy transplanting begins in Assam. Famous for the annual Ambubachi Mela at Kamakhya Temple.

Month 4

Saon (শাওণ)

Monsoon (Barsa)
Gregorian: Mid-July – Mid-August

Peak monsoon month. Sacred for Shiva worship and Manasa Puja across Assamese households.

Month 5

Bhado (ভাদ)

Early Autumn (Sarat)
Gregorian: Mid-August – Mid-September

Sacred month marked by Janmashtami, Srimanta Sankardeva Tithi, and Sri Madhavdeva Tithi with Naam-Kirtan in Namghars.

Month 6

Ahin (আহিন)

Autumn (Sarat)
Gregorian: Mid-September – Mid-October

Autumnal breezes and clear skies. Celebrates Durga Puja and sets the stage for Kati Bihu.

Month 7

Kati (কাতি)

Late Autumn (Hemanta)
Gregorian: Mid-October – Mid-November

Month of Kati Bihu (Kongali Bihu). Earthen lamps (Saki) lit at the foot of Tulsi plants and in paddy fields praying for crop protection.

Month 8

Aghon (আঘোণ)

Late Autumn (Hemanta)
Gregorian: Mid-November – Mid-December

Harvest season. Golden rice fields, harvesting paddy, and celebrating Na-Khowa (feast of newly harvested rice).

Month 9

Puh (পুহ)

Winter (Sita)
Gregorian: Mid-December – Mid-January

Crisp winter month. Families prepare Bira (rice cakes), larus, and firewood for Magh Bihu.

Month 10

Magh (মাঘ)

Winter (Sita)
Gregorian: Mid-January – Mid-February

Month of Magh Bihu (Bhogali Bihu). Meji bonfires, Uruka feast night, pitha making, and community sports.

Month 11

Phagun (ফাগুন)

Spring (Basanta)
Gregorian: Mid-February – Mid-March

Transition into spring. Celebrates Doul Utsav / Fagua (Holi) with fervor and colorful natural powders.

Month 12

Chait (চ’ত)

Spring (Basanta)
Gregorian: Mid-March – Mid-April

Final month of the Bhaskarabda year. Preparing Husori troupes and instruments for Bohag Bihu.

What Is the Assamese Calendar (Bhaskarabda)?

The Assamese calendar is the regional lunisolar calendar used in the state of Assam, India. It is dated according to the Bhaskarabda era, named in honor of King Bhaskaravarman of Kamarupa (600–650 CE), who was a contemporary of King Harshavardhana.

The Assamese year begins on Bohag 1 (mid-April), which marks the spring festival of Rongali Bihu. Because the calendar follows solar transits across sidereal zodiac signs, each month starts when the Sun enters a new Rashi (Sankranti).

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the Assamese calendar 2026–2027?▼

The Assamese calendar 2026–2027 is the traditional solar regional calendar used in Assam, India. It spans the Bhaskarabda years 1433 and 1434, marking regional months like Bohag, Magh, Kati, and festivals such as Bohag Bihu and Ambubachi Mela alongside Gregorian dates.

What Assamese year (Bhaskarabda) corresponds to 2026?▼

The year 2026 corresponds primarily to Bhaskarabda 1433 (which runs until mid-April 2026) and Bhaskarabda 1434 (which begins on Bohag Bihu / April 15, 2027).

When is Bohag Bihu in 2026?▼

Bohag Bihu 2026 begins on April 14, 2026 (Goru Bihu) and continues with Manuh Bihu (Assamese New Year) on April 15, 2026.

When is Magh Bihu in 2027?▼

Magh Bihu 2027 (Bhogali Bihu) takes place on January 14–15, 2027, starting with Uruka feast night on January 14 followed by Meji bonfires on January 15.

What are the 12 Assamese months?▼

The 12 Assamese months are Bohag (বহাগ), Jeth (জেঠ), Ahar (আহাৰ), Saon (শাওণ), Bhado (ভাদ), Ahin (আহিন), Kati (কাতি), Aghon (আঘোণ), Puh (পুহ), Magh (মাঘ), Phagun (ফাগুন), and Chait (চ’ত).

How is the Assamese calendar different from the Gregorian calendar?▼

The Assamese calendar is a solar sidereal calendar calculated from the movement of the Sun across the 12 zodiac signs (Rashi), whereas the Gregorian calendar is a tropical solar calendar. Assamese months begin around the middle of Gregorian months.

What is Bhaskarabda?▼

Bhaskarabda is the traditional Assamese era named after King Bhaskaravarman of Kamarupa (600–650 CE). It counts years starting approximately 593 years after the Gregorian era.
